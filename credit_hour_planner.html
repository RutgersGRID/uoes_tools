Credit Hour Planner

Use this tool to determine the total time needed for all of your planned course activities.

Hints on how to use this tool.

This tool is designed to help you plan your course by estimating the total time needed for all of your planned course activities.

Enter the credit hours for your course.

Enter the semester length in weeks.

Select your course format (all synchronous meetings, mixed synchronous and self-paced work, or all self-paced work).

List the activities in your course — how many of each, and how long each one takes a student — and mark each one as instructional time or out-of-class work.

Check whether the whole course adds up to the time its credit hours require.

Click a section heading to expand or collapse that section.

Your work will be saved automatically as you go.

Why we built this tool:

This tool is designed to help you determine the expected total time needed for all of your planned course activities and ensure your plan meets federal, state, and Rutgers guidelines. For all three, a minimum of 1 hour of instructional time and 2 hours of student work is required per credit per week in a 15 week semester. However 50 minutes of instructional time is treated as an hour.

For a 3 credit course, this comes to a total of 37.5 hours of instructional time and 75 hours of student work or 112.5 hours total.
Across a 15 week semester, this comes out to 2.5 hours of instructional time and 5 hours of student work per week.

A shorter term carries the same total requirements which will require more work per week.

Code of Federal Regulations - 34 C.F.R. § 600.2 (2026)
New Jersey Administrative Code - N.J.A.C. 9A:1-1.2
Rutgers Policy 10.2.4

Note: These minimums are based on federal and state guidelines -- your department may have additional requirements.

How these minimums are calculated.

A "credit hour" is at least 50 minutes of faculty-led instruction and another 100 minutes of out-of-class student work, each week, for 15 weeks.

Multiply the number of credits by the minimums to determine the total amount of work required for the course.

For shorter terms, the minimum work required per week is adjusted to meet the total requirements across the course. Divide 15 by the number of weeks in the term to get the adjustment factor. A 5 week term is 3 times shorter than a 15 week term, so the minimum work required per week would be 3 times greater.

References: 34 CFR § 600.2, New Jersey Administrative Code Title 9A., Rutgers University Policy 10.2.4

What is, and isn't, instructional time?

Instructional time is the structured, instructor-led learning that takes place during scheduled meetings or through asynchronous materials that are monitored and documented. This includes, but is not limited to, live meetings, lab or field work, internships, and other forms of experiential learning.

Asynchronous materials that can track student engagement also count towards instructional time. This can be recorded lectures, guided activities, or instructor-directed content that students work through on their own time.

This is distinct from homework or out-of-class study.

For example, a 1.5-hour class and a 1-hour WeVideo lecture each week would be 2.5 hours of instructional time a week.

What is out-of-class work?

Out-of-class work is what students do on their own time without direct instruction, supervision or monitoring: reading, writing and other assignments, studying, and preparing for exams.

The credit hour asks for at least twice as much of it as instructional time — 100 minutes a week for every credit in a 15-week term.

The same activity can land on either side. A discussion an instructor monitors and documents is instructional time; one students complete on their own is out-of-class work. Choose whichever describes how your course runs it.
