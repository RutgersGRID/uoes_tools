How this is calculated

One credit hour means one 50-minute period of instruction plus the study time you set above, each week, for a 15-week semester.

What counts as instructional time

The instructional row is seat time — the 50-minute period per credit hour a course meets for. A 3-credit course owes 150 minutes a week.

In a traditional course that is literally class meeting time. In a fully online course there is no room, so it is the asynchronous equivalent: instructor-directed content — recorded lectures, guided activities, anything standing in for a class meeting.

In a blended course it splits. The face-to-face row is what you actually meet for; the online instructional row is the seat time your meetings do not cover.

It is not homework. Out-of-class work is the studying row.

The acceleration rate

A course worth the same credits has to deliver the same total hours no matter how long the term runs. The acceleration rate is 15 ÷ your semester length.

A 15-week term has a rate of 1.0. A 7-week term has a rate of about 2.14, so every week carries roughly twice the work.

Face-to-face meetings are the exception: they stay at their actual scheduled length. In an accelerated blended course the extra instructional time that the meetings do not cover is pushed into the online instructional total.
