Course basics

Based on the entered credits (3) and course length (15 weeks), the course needs at least 37 hours and 30 minutes of instructional time and 75 hours of out-of-class work, 112 hours and 30 minutes in all. Across 15 weeks that averages 2 hours and 30 minutes of instructional time and 5 hours of out-of-class work a week.

Note: These minimums are based on federal and state guidelines -- your department may have additional requirements.

How these minimums are calculated.

A "credit hour" is at least 50 minutes of faculty-led instruction and another 100 minutes of out-of-class student work, each week, for 15 weeks.

Multiply the number of credits by the minimums to determine the total amount of work required for the course.

For shorter terms, the minimum work required per week is adjusted to meet the total requirements across the course. Divide 15 by the number of weeks in the term to get the adjustment factor. A 5 week term is 3 times shorter than a 15 week term, so the minimum work required per week would be 3 times greater.

References: 34 CFR § 600.2, New Jersey Administrative Code Title 9A., Rutgers University Policy 10.2.4

Some scheduled meetings, with the rest of the instructional time self-paced.

Choosing Synchronous or Blended / hybrid will create a Class meetings line in Course activities below.

What is, and isn't, instructional time?

Instructional time is the structured, instructor-led learning that takes place during scheduled meetings or through asynchronous materials that are monitored and documented. This includes, but is not limited to, live meetings (in person or online), lab or field work, internships, and other forms of experiential learning with direct instructor involvement.

Asynchronous materials that can track student engagement also count towards instructional time. This can be recorded lectures, guided activities, or instructor-directed content that students work through on their own time.

Out-of-class work is distinct from instructional time. It is what students do on their own time without direct instruction, supervision or monitoring: homework such as reading, writing and other assignments, studying, and preparing for exams.

The credit hour asks for at least twice as much out-of-class work as instructional time — 100 minutes a week for every credit in a 15-week term.

The same activity can land on either side. A discussion an instructor monitors and documents is instructional time; one students complete on their own is out-of-class work. Choose whichever describes how your course runs it.

Choosing a type fills in an estimated time for that activity, which you can change. Estimated times are intended to be a starting point, not a requirement or exact amount.
