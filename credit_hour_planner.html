Credit Hour Planner

Use this tool to determine the total time needed for all of your planned course activities.

Hints on how to use this tool.

This tool helps you plan your course by estimating the total time needed for all of your planned course activities.

Select your course format (all synchronous meetings, mixed synchronous and self-paced work, or all self-paced work).

Enter the semester length in weeks.

Enter all your planned activities and the time you expect students to spend on each.

Turn your course’s credit hours into an hours-per-week budget, then check whether the activities you have planned for a module actually fit inside it.

Click a section heading to expand or collapse that section.

Your work will be saved automatically as you go.

Why we built this tool:

This tool is designed to help you determine the expected total time needed for all of your planned course activities and ensure your plan meets federal, state, and Rutgers guidelines. For all three, a minimum of 1 hour of instructional time and 2 hours of student work is required per credit per week in a 15 week semester. However 50 minutes of instructional/academic time is treated as an hour.

For a 3 credit course, this comes to a total of 37.5 hours of instructional or academic time and 75 hours of student work or 112.5 hours total.
Across a 15 week semester, this comes out to 2.5 hours of instructional time and 5 hours of student work per week.

A shorter term carries the same total requirements which will require more work per week.

Code of Federal Regulations - 34 C.F.R. § 600.2 (2026)
New Jersey Administrative Code - N.J.A.C. 9A:1-1.2
Rutgers Policy 10.2.4

Based on the entered credits (3) and course length (15 weeks), the minimum required academic work is 150 minutes (2 hours and 30 minutes) per week and the minimum additional student work is 300 minutes (5 hours) per week, for a total of 37 hours and 30 minutes of academic work and 75 hours of additional student work across the entire course.

Note: These minimums are based on federal and state guidelines -- your department may have additional requirements.

How these minimums are calculated.

A "credit hour" is at least 50 minutes of faculty-led instruction and another 100 minutes of out-of-class student work, each week, for 15 weeks.

Multiply the number of credits by the minimums to determine the total amount of work required for the course.

For shorter terms, the minimum work required per week is adjusted to meet the total requirements across the course. Divide 15 by the number of weeks in the term to get the adjustment factor. A 5 week term is 3 times shorter than a 15 week term, so the minimum work required per week would be 3 times greater.

References: 34 CFR § 600.2, New Jersey Administrative Code Title 9A., Rutgers University Policy 10.2.4

What counts as instructional time

The instructional rows are seat time — the 50-minute period per credit hour a course owes each week. A 3-credit course owes 150 minutes.

Your planned academic work covers it directly — scheduled meetings, where a live online meeting counts the same as one in a room, together with asynchronous material whose time can be monitored and documented. Whatever that does not cover is self-paced: the asynchronous equivalent of a class meeting — recorded lectures, guided activities, instructor-directed content students work through on their own time.

Planning more academic work than the course carries credits is fine. Labs, studios and clinicals routinely do, and the federal definition sets the credit hour as a minimum rather than a ceiling. The self-paced instructional row simply drops to zero.

It is not homework. Out-of-class work is the studying row.

Reading, assignments, studying — work students do on their own.

Enter the out-of-class work you expect students to do in a week, in minutes. The statutory minimum is 100 minutes per credit; many departments plan for 150.

How this is calculated

One credit hour means one 50-minute period of instruction plus the study time you set above, each week, for a 15-week semester.

The acceleration rate

A course worth the same credits has to deliver the same total hours no matter how long the term runs. The acceleration rate is 15 ÷ your semester length.

A 15-week term has a rate of 1.0. A 7-week term has a rate of about 2.14, so every week carries roughly twice the work.

Planned academic work is the exception: it stays at the length you entered. In an accelerated course the extra instructional time that it does not cover is pushed into the self-paced instructional total.

What counts as Academic Work

Enter the week's scheduled meetings in minutes, including instruction, labs, or other scheduled academic work. Asynchronous learning activities using technology that can monitor and document the time students participate can be included in this number. For example, a 1.5 hour class and 1 hour WeVideo lecture, each week, would be 2.5 hours of academic work.
